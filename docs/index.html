02

Fundamental history: five regimes, one asset

Bitcoin's price has been driven by a different marginal buyer in every era. Understanding the current fall requires knowing which regime broke.

2014–2016

Post-Mt.Gox winter

Retail-only market recovering from exchange failure. Near-zero correlation with any macro variable — our regressions confirm no NASDAQ beta and no rate sensitivity in this era.

2017–2018

The ICO bubble

Retail speculation drove BTC from ~$1k to $19.7k, then −84%. China's exchange ban cost 9% in a week; the actual top came months later from leverage exhaustion, not regulation.

2020–2021

The liquidity cycle

Zero rates, QE and the first institutional wave took BTC to $69k — and permanently converted it into a macro asset. Rolling correlation with NASDAQ jumped from ~0 to 0.4+ and never went back.

2022

Tightening plus structural collapse

The fastest hiking cycle in 40 years repriced all risk, then crypto-native leverage imploded: Terra (−12% in 7 days), FTX (−18% in 7 days), −77% peak to trough. Structural failures hurt far more than any government ever has.

2023–2025

The ETF era

SVB's failure was Bitcoin's best 30-day event window ever measured here (+39%). Spot ETF approval rerouted institutional flow; the halving, a pro-crypto US election (+42% in 30 days) and record inflows carried price to $126k by October 2025.

October 2025 → now

The current fall: trigger, flows, macro

Trigger: the 10 Oct 2025 tariff shock hit record leverage — ~$19B liquidated in a day, order-book depth collapsed and never recovered. Flows: spot ETFs flipped to record outflows (~$3.3B out in H1 2026; a record 13-day streak in June) and Strategy's disclosed BTC sale broke treasury-company confidence. Macro: hawkish Fed repricing, a strong dollar, and rotation into AI equities. Two consecutive losing quarters to open the year — previously seen only in 2018 and 2022.

The factor regressions

Weekly BTC log returns regressed on NASDAQ, gold and dollar-index returns, the change in the 10-year yield, and the change in the Fear & Greed index (OLS, Newey-West errors). Three windows: full sample, pre-ETF, and the ETF era.

The result is unambiguous: Bitcoin trades as a high-beta tech-risk asset. NASDAQ beta ≈ 0.5 is the only macro factor consistently significant, and it rose after the ETFs arrived — institutionalization made Bitcoin more macro, not less (R² 0.34 → 0.42). The "digital gold" beta is statistically zero and turned negative in the ETF era.

Bold = significant at 5%. F&G change scaled to [0,1]. HAC standard errors, 4 lags. Sample limited by F&G availability (Feb 2018).

Rolling 90-day correlation of daily BTC returns

The structural break is visible: pre-2020 Bitcoin was uncorrelated with everything. Since 2020 the NASDAQ correlation has been persistently positive, spiking toward 0.6–0.8 in every stress window — including the current one.

06

Interest rates, money supply, and governments

Rates: daily and weekly yield-change betas are statistically insignificant in every era — Bitcoin does not trade tick-for-tick with treasuries. But the two worst years in its institutional life (2022, 2026) are both hawkish-repricing years: rates act through regime and risk appetite, not through daily co-movement. That distinction matters — it means no rate level kills Bitcoin, but a hawkish surprise cycle has always coincided with its bear markets since 2020.

Governments: measured event by event, hostile state action has never been fatal — and the market's response has decayed with each repetition.

09

The verdict

The bear case — all empirically live

  • The marginal buyer (spot ETFs) is now a structural seller — record outflow streaks through June 2026
  • Hawkish Fed repricing underway; BTC has never bottomed during one
  • NASDAQ beta ~0.5 and rising: no diversification when you need it
  • Two losing quarters to open the year — only 2018 and 2022 match, both full bears
  • Order-book depth never recovered from Oct 10; thin markets fall faster
  • Prediction markets price ~52% odds of sub-$50k in 2026

The bull case — equally empirical

  • Price at the 200-week moving average — the best accumulation zone in all four prior cycles
  • Fear & Greed at 11: the extreme-fear bucket averages +5.0% fwd 30d with a 63% hit rate
  • Hash rate at 78% of ATH: the security budget and miner base are intact
  • Selling is flow-driven (ETF redemptions, treasury unwinds), not a protocol or credibility failure — historically the recoverable kind
  • Every prior ≥50% drawdown resolved to new highs — sample of four
  • Mayer multiple 0.80: bottom decile of its historical distribution
Synthesis

Statistically, Bitcoin's price is ~40–50% explained by two observables: tech-equity risk appetite and its own reflexive sentiment. Neither has turned. Valuation says risk/reward for a multi-year holder is better than at any point since late 2022 — but both prior analogs bottomed only after the macro cycle turned: the 2019 Fed pivot, the 2022 peak-inflation moment.

The data supports treating this as a staged-accumulation zone for multi-year capital that can tolerate a further −20–30% leg — not a momentum entry. The single most informative catalyst to watch is ETF flows turning positive, because the entire 2024–25 repricing was built on them.
