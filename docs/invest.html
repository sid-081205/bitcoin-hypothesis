1. You are being offered the historical "buy zone" price

Bitcoin is sitting right at its 200-week moving average — basically the average price of the last four years. This level has only been reached four times before: 2015, 2018, 2020 and 2022. Every single time, it marked the cheap part of the cycle, and everyone who bought there and waited a few years made money. You don't get this price when things feel good — that's exactly why it's available now.

2. Everyone is scared, and that has been a good sign

The Fear & Greed index is at 11 out of 100 — deep "extreme fear." We tested what happened after every extreme-fear day since 2018 (326 of them): on average Bitcoin was up +5.0% thirty days later, and it was positive 63% of the time. Buying when others panic has been a mildly but consistently winning trade.

3. Nothing is actually broken

This matters more than anything. The bad crashes in Bitcoin's history came with something breaking: exchanges collapsing (Mt.Gox, FTX), fake coins imploding (Terra). This time, nothing like that has happened. The selling is coming from ETF investors taking money out and one company selling some coins. Meanwhile the network itself is fine: the computing power securing Bitcoin (hash rate) is still at 78% of its all-time high through a 52% price drop. Miners haven't given up. The thing itself works exactly as it did at $126k.

4. Bitcoin has survived worse — five times

Bitcoin has fallen 50% or more five times in its life, including two crashes deeper than 75%. It recovered to new all-time highs after every single one. It has survived China banning it three separate times (after the third ban in 2021, price was actually up 36% a month later), the US suing its biggest exchanges, and endless "Bitcoin is dead" headlines. An asset that governments actively tried to kill and couldn't is showing you something about its durability.

5. The selling pressure has a visible end

Prices fall when there are more sellers than buyers — and right now the sellers are mostly ETF redemptions. That's actually good news in disguise: it's a measurable, watchable flow, not an invisible structural rot. ETF outflows already hit record streaks in June; selling of that intensity historically exhausts itself. And the macro pressure (rate-hike fears, strong dollar) is cyclical — the Fed has never stayed hawkish forever. In 2018 and 2022, the moment macro turned, Bitcoin bottomed and roughly tripled within 18 months.

6. The math of buying low

Simple arithmetic: if Bitcoin merely returns to its old high of $124,753 — a level it traded at just nine months ago — that is roughly a +107% gain from today's $60,357. For it to lose the same amount, it would have to go to zero, which would require the failure of a network that has run without interruption for 17 years. Past cycles took 2–3 years to reclaim highs. Nobody can promise that repeats — but you are being paid, in expected value, for tolerating the fear.

"The time to buy is when there's blood in the streets." Every prior Bitcoin cycle rewarded exactly this — buying at the 200-week average, during extreme fear, when the headlines said it was over.
The honest fine print

This case only works if two things are true about you: your money can stay invested for 3+ years, and you can watch it drop another 20–30% without selling. In 2018 and 2022, prices fell a further 15–25% below the 200-week average before turning. Buy in stages, not all at once — and only with money you don't need.
